# Tower vs high-mast lighting pole

**Site:** Houston County EOC, 102 Chief Williams Drive, Kathleen, GA 31047  
**Use:** New 80 ft AGL support for two 5.8 GHz AREDN dishes (027.5° true NNE to Houston Medical, 195.3° true SSW to Emory Hospital Perry)  
**Date:** November 2026

**Recommendation:** Use the **antenna tower** (tilt-over / light lattice) unless the county mandates a lighting-pole look and will staff a climber or pay for bucket trucks.

Line-item dollars: `EOC-tower-vs-high-mast-pole-cost.md`.

## Side-by-side

| | **Antenna tower** (tilt-over / light lattice) | **High-mast lighting pole** (no lights, no winch) |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Comms mast built for dishes | Tapered galvanized lighting shaft used as a stick |
| **Installed cost** | ~$12,000–$26,000 | ~$19,000–$39,000 bare; ~$22,000–$46,000 with climb kit |
| **Typical midpoint** | ~$18,000 | ~$28,000 bare; ~$32,000 with pegs |
| **Foundation / crane** | Smaller pad; often no crane | Deep caisson; crane to stack sections |
| **Dish height** | 80 ft centerline is the spec | 80 ft pole often yields only 65–70 ft if dishes sit below the top |
| **Wind / PE** | Sized for antenna sail (8–12 sq ft); may need XHD for EOC Risk Category III | Lighting catalog is often Risk Category II; PE will likely upsize for dishes + climber |
| **Climb / service** | Tilt down; work on the ground | Smooth shaft — not climbable unless you add pegs |
| **Climbing pegs** | Not needed | Extra $800–$4,000 factory steps, plus OSHA cable |
| **Safety climb** | Not needed | Extra $400–$1,200 cable + grab; harness ~$325–$500 |
| **Each dish alignment** | ~$0 (ARES / county staff) | Qualified climber, or bucket $800–$1,500 |
| **Two opposite dishes** | Cross-arm at the top (027.5° NNE and 195.3° SSW) | Custom arm; lighting tenon is useless |
| **Look / footprint** | Looks like a ham/comms tower | Familiar county/DOT look; no guys |
| **Cabling** | Clipped or internal Cat6 | Cat6 can run inside the pole |
| **Best when** | RF, cost, and aiming matter | County requires a lighting-pole look and will staff a climber |

## Advantages and disadvantages

### Antenna tower

**Advantages**

- Built for antennas: wind area, cross-arm, and 80 ft dish centerline
- Tilt down for install and aiming — no climber, no bucket
- Lighter foundation; often no crane
- Usually cheaper installed
- Easy Cat6, grounding, and two opposite dishes
- ARES/county staff can realign without a lighting contractor

**Disadvantages**

- Looks like a ham/comms tower, not a parking-lot light
- Aluminum tilt-overs (Aluma HN class) may need XHD / PE upsize for EOC wind
- Guyed kits need more yard; freestanding kits need a hinge-base pad
- Not a lighting structure — site lights stay separate

### High-mast lighting pole

**Advantages**

- Familiar to county/DOT: galvanized tapered steel, small footprint
- Durable shaft and finish if PE-sized
- Single stick, no guy wires
- Cat6 can run inside the pole
- Factory climbing pegs available (extra cost)

**Disadvantages**

- Not designed for two 30 dBi dishes — PE will likely upsize steel and foundation
- Cannot tilt; stock pole cannot be climbed
- Climbing pegs plus OSHA cable-grab add ~$2,500–$7,000, plus a qualified climber
- Needs a crane and a deep caisson — usually more expensive even with no lights and no winch
- Lighting tenon is useless; still buy a custom dish arm
- An 80 ft light pole often only gives 65–70 ft of RF height if dishes sit below the top
- Every 5.8 GHz tweak is a climb or an $800–$1,500 bucket
- Field-drilling pegs can cut capacity and void galvanizing
